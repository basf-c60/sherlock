B405: Copy the typed objects into the two object slots, in the verb's order
Once a verb record has matched (B39D): works out from the record (A2BD, $A01D/$A01E) and the preposition typed whether the parse node's first or second group of words is the direct object, notes the order at $B182, and copies six bytes of words from the node into the direct-object slot $B172 and the indirect-object slot $B178, setting bit 0 of B16D/$B16E when a slot is not empty.
TAKE LAMP: no preposition, $A01E = $40, BC = $0812 - the direct object from the node's first group (+8), the indirect from the second (+18). GIVE WATSON LAMP: no preposition, $A01E = $61 (bit 5 set), BC = $1208 - the other way round, and the game says 'You give the oil lamp to Watson.' GIVE LAMP TO WATSON: TO matches the node's first group's preposition (+14), $A01D = $0C, bit 5 flipped, BC = $0812. TALK TO WATSON: TO typed ($B188) but not attached to a group, so $A01D's bit 5 ($28) is used as it is: $0812.
How it works:
1. If the pattern matched with its middle words swapped (A = 1), swap the two prepositions at $B188 and $B18A.
2. Gather the verb's flags (A2BD).
3. With no preposition typed, bit 5 of $A01E decides the order of the two noun phrases (GIVE WATSON LAMP).
4. Otherwise see which of the node's two groups the preposition belongs to (+14 or +16) and put each group in the matching slot.
Input
A 1 if the pattern matched with its middle words swapped, 0 if not
IY The parse node
Output
The two object slots filled in the verb's order
