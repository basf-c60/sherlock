AD57: Count the sentence, then on to the next or finish
Bumps the sentence counter A012 (not for a comma in mode 2 of A01A); a comma ($A0) goes back to read another sentence (AABD).
EXAMINE LAMP AND YOUR ARMCHAIR and DROP LAMP AND YOUR MONEY each run as two commands, the verb carried to the second; but GIVE LAMP AND YOUR MONEY TO WATSON (and with a comma for AND) gets I SEE NOTHING THAT YOU CAN GIVE, and PUT LAMP AND YOUR MONEY IN YOUR ARMCHAIR gets WHAT ?, though GIVE YOUR MONEY TO WATSON on its own works. The backward pass at AE1E, which copies words from a later sentence into an earlier one with the same verb, never compares the last pair - see the Bugs page ('and-to').
How it works:
1. Count the sentence in A012, except for a comma in statement mode.
2. A comma ($A0) goes back to read another sentence (AABD).
3. If a line of several sentences is being worked through ($A01B set): if the second node has no verb of its own, carry its verb flag into the first node and fill the first node's empty word slots from the second (AE59). Then drop the first node of the list.
4. Go through the sentences from the start. In a statement whose third group is empty, take the previous sentence's group. A sentence with no verb is merged into the previous one (AEF1).
5. Then go backwards from the last sentence: where an earlier sentence has the same verb as a later one and no second group of its own, copy the later sentence's second group into it (AF27). So EXAMINE LAMP AND YOUR ARMCHAIR is understood as two commands with the same verb. The loop never compares the last pair; see the Bugs page.
6. Return Z: done.
Input
D The token class last read
E Flags
Output
F Z when the sentences have been tidied
