A2E4: Narrate a command as a sentence ('You take the oil lamp.')
Saves the verb and objects (A35D-A35F), then - unless it is too dark and the player is the one acting, when it prints 'SORRY IT IS TOO DARK.' (68D1) - prints the actor (A724), the verb's words from its pattern (A2B1/A2BD) and the objects, ending the line (A2A2). This is the sentence every successful action shows, for the player and for characters in sight ('Inspector Lestrade hails the hansom cab.').
What $A00C means (the B of this routine) was not established, so the two narration paths are described by the branch, not by who is acting.
How it works:
1. Set A2AA to 1 while narrating, and keep the verb and both objects at A35D to A35F.
2. Work out whether the text is printed as quoted ($A027 is 1 when $A00C is 0).
3. If it is too dark to see (D078) and the actor is the player, print 'SORRY IT IS TOO DARK.' and finish.
4. Find the verb's record and gather its flags (A2BD). If bit 4 of $A01D is set the verb is never narrated.
5. Start a fresh line if the actor is a character other than the player, Watson or Lestrade's usual set (DC6C), and print the actor's name (A724).
6. If the actor was the player print the word YOU and narrate the command (A360); otherwise narrate it as it stands (A37D). End the line, clear A2AA and $A027.
Input
$A033 The verb
A034 The first object, A035 the second
$A036 The actor
Output
All registers kept
