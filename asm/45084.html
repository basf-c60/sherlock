B01C: Handlers for BUT, ALL and a quote mark
B01C (BUT, after ALL) and B033 (ALL) set A01A to 2 and 1 for 'all but' and 'all'. B049 (a quote mark): the words that follow are speech; their position is added to the list at 9FEC and the parser skips to the closing quote, marking it as the end of a sentence. B86A hands them on when SAY TO / TALK TO runs. B09A (PAUSE) waits (C415). B0A3 (LOAD, and QUIT too) goes to B0AC: the game asks for a saved-game tape.
QUIT and LOAD both print 'PLAY SAVED GAME TAPE TO BE LOADED' and set $A0DA to 1; PAUSE and NOPRINT are accepted with no message.
A line that is only a quotation, "HELLO", is taken as TALK (the word at $088B): 'You talk to Watson.' and Watson's good morning, the same as SAY "HELLO". TO WATSON "HELLO" gets WHAT ?.
Each quotation in a SAY TO / TALK TO is added to the list at 9FEC (B049) whether or not the command later succeeds; B86A hands on the first node. A SAY that failed (I SEE NO ... THAT YOU CAN TALK TO, no answer) therefore leaves a stale quotation in front of the next one, and the next person spoken to hears the old words. TALK TO <person> with no quotation empties the list.
